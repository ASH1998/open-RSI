# Prospective MBPP-only lesson-selection test

**Decision: offline design only.** The available records support an audit of lesson content and a matched-input dry run. They provide zero isolated lesson-choice outcomes from the completed child pair. A learned selector needs outcomes in which changing a lesson changes what a matched student learns.

## A concrete comparison

Give two copies of the same student seven identical verified lessons, in identical positions, plus one alternative lesson in the last position. Use one common teacher's candidate pool, so teacher identity cannot explain the difference. A first example can be built entirely from previously exposed MBPP training tasks. It is development material.

| Bundle | Seven common MBPP lessons | Eighth MBPP lesson | Processed tokens per pass | Supervised tokens per pass |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| A | 662, 664, 688, 744, 759, 796, 832 | 723 · count equal values at corresponding positions in two lists | 1,339 | 243 |
| B | same ordered seven | 811 · decide whether two lists of tuples are identical | 1,338 | 240 |

Both options teach a collections problem. The swap changes one lesson while keeping processed and supervised token totals each within a proposed 5% tolerance. All answers in this example come from the historical learned teacher. Neither bundle contains the quarantined task 663. The checksum and exact selected answer for each slot can be reproduced with the dry-run command. No student has been trained on either bundle; neither option has an observed advantage.

## How to learn and test the choice

1. **Collect paired decisions for selector training.** Freeze multiple disjoint lesson-choice pairs and development probes before seeing their student scores. For each pair, train two copies of the same starting checkpoint on the two alternatives. Randomize the assignment of choices to worker slots and use the same adapter initialization, ordering, update count, hardware and evaluator. Record both prompt and supervised tokens. Each student receives the same evaluation, including the unchanged baseline student's per-task answers. Keep ties and regressions.
2. **Define a defensible training target.** Compare the two students' development passes on tasks outside the lesson pool. Give preference to an option only when it gains more tasks and loses no additional baseline retention successes. Mark conflicting tradeoffs or equal outcomes unranked. Keep the raw paired outcomes. Fit a small ranking policy on the informative training comparisons, using only features observable before a choice, such as lesson category, token length, repair provenance and baseline skill on a separate diagnostic block. Exclude teacher ID, held-back outcomes and hidden test contents from selector inputs. If comparisons offer too few informative preferences to fit and validate a policy, stop before the selector test.
3. **Compare the frozen learned selector to its original rule.** Fix the original rule before outcome collection, for example category-balanced selection with a deterministic hash tie-breaker. Give both policies identical previously unseen options, starting students, permitted observations and token budgets. Separate selector-training, selector-development and held-back policy-test task families or semantic clusters; also separate the lessons from the student probes within each episode. Neither test choices nor test scores train the selector. Identical choices contribute an observed zero difference rather than being silently dropped.
4. **Evaluate successor-making ability.** Score the two resulting students on the same blinded fresh MBPP development tasks and the same retention probes. Compare per-task outcomes against each other and the starting student. Before spending, freeze the sample size and detectable improvement, the allowed retention losses, treatment of ties, and replication seeds. A first candidate gate is at least three net fresh gains over both comparators, no individual baseline or comparator retention success lost, followed by independent replication. An eventual recursive test requires the successor to produce another better improver under the same controls. This proposal targets the preceding one-step measurement.

## Feasibility gates before any launch

- **Fresh MBPP coverage.** Deduplicating the 284 earlier exposure records plus 24 repair training and nine evaluation rows gives 317 unique IDs, including 287 MBPP IDs. The 16 repair retention IDs were already in the prior registry. The 27 admitted native MBPP rows were all exposed by repair-v1. Of the six screened validation candidates, 531 and 575 reached further review. Both passed renewed checks against the 317 exposure IDs, source rows and duplicate families. Their pinned references passed six native assertions and six independently specified cases in total. The follow-up screen quarantined 531 because it shares the coin-change domain and two of its three native inputs with exposed task 918. Only 575 remains for bounded development, with no text task. Its pinned reference matched an independent range oracle on 3,522 valid calls. The other 37 unselected native training candidates have recorded exclusion reasons. The current reviewed inventory cannot support a fresh multi-task test of the proposed selector. Keep all final rows sealed and preserve the native-only source choice. [Native screening record](../repair_v1/data/selection.json)
- **Outcome evidence.** The historical pair changed multiple examples together and tied on all 25 task-level outcomes. It supplies no per-lesson preference target. Do not label the longer dataset better, infer lesson rewards from teacher pass counts, or fit a selector to the tied child outcomes. Future preference collection needs multiple independent pairs and a protected test set before fitting starts.
- **Compute.** The ledger leaves $6 unallocated. The previous two child stages alone required $10 in reservations. A matched lesson-pair cost can be estimated later, but a new stage plan must include both arms, evaluation, failures and replication, and fit the existing reservation policy. Only the main project chat coordinates paid launches.
- **Decision to reconsider.** First identify and independently verify a sufficiently large, legitimately unused **MBPP-only** student evaluation block under the same duplicate rules. Then estimate the number of paired trials and seeds needed for the chosen effect size and prepare an all-stage reservation plan. If those conditions cannot be met, keep the bundles as a development example and retain the original parent.

## Feasibility run

The [saved decision](feasibility.json) is **stop before training**. On this screened block, one remaining fresh task permits at most one net gain even if the comparator fails it. The proposed scientific threshold requires three. The 3,522 checks for MBPP 575 enumerate divisors 2 through 8, lower endpoints 1 through 10, upper endpoints from the lower endpoint through ten above it, and every reachable positive rank. These are checks of one trusted reference, not 3,522 independent tasks or student scores.

Using the last study's reservation sizes, one probe ($1), one fresh baseline ($4), and two student training/evaluation stages ($5 each) require $15 for a first pair. This planning scenario is $9 beyond the $6 unallocated. It excludes later pairs and replication. A lower-cost controller would need its own justified bounds and main-chat coordination; changing the budget alone cannot fix the evaluation ceiling. No reservation was made. The native final and prompt splits stayed sealed.
